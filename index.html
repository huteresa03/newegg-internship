UI/UX Designer — from ticket to live on site

  • AI-assisted workflow. I used Claude Code daily to scaffold and build landing pages, plus AI image tools for campaign art — then refined everything by hand.

  • Designed in Figma first — desktop and mobile frames, an icon system for the how-it-works steps, and partner logo placement (Intel, AMD, NVIDIA).
  • Hand-built the page — responsive HTML/CSS with live hardware value tables (CPU/GPU lists customers can check before trading in).
  • Shipped, then revised — a second ticket (26-0737) updated content and layout after launch feedback. Real product work: nothing is ever "done once."

09 · Key Learnings & Skills

What this summer taught me

  • AI is a daily tool, not a gimmick. I learned a new AI capability almost every day — Claude Code for building landing pages, generative imagery for campaign art — and learned where human judgment must take over.
  • Design-to-code fluency. Owning a page from Figma frame to responsive HTML/CSS makes you a better designer — you design what can actually ship.
  • Production discipline. Brand guidelines, safe areas, retina exports, file naming — craft at scale is a skill of its own.
  • UX research in practice. Auditing the PSU Calculator and writing a real PRD showed me how research turns into a buildable plan.
  • Working inside a system. Newegg's design system and each partner's brand rules taught me creativity within constraints.
  • Communication is half the job. Tickets, feedback rounds, and marketing-manager reviews taught me to present and defend design decisions.

11 · For Future Interns

If you want to intern at Newegg…

  • Bring production skills, not just portfolios. Photoshop speed and clean file discipline matter from day one — pretty concepts only count when they ship in 17 sizes.
  • Learn HTML/CSS and AI tools before you arrive. A designer who can build (or direct AI to build) a page is dramatically more valuable — this was my biggest edge.
  • Treat every ticket as a case study. Even a banner set teaches hierarchy, brand systems, and stakeholder feedback. Document as you go — your portfolio writes itself.
  • Ask for the bigger projects. The UX audit and landing pages happened because I raised my hand. Companies give real work to interns who ask for it.
